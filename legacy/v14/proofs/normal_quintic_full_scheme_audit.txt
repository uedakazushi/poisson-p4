V14 FINAL INDEPENDENT AUDIT: NORMAL-QUINTIC FULL SCHEME ISOMORPHISM
================================================================
Audited manuscript: sections/normal_quintics.tex, especially the section
"正規退化因子をもつ部分の全スキーム表示" and Theorem thm:normal-scheme.
Conclusion: the stated isomorphism is valid on the specified open loci WITHOUT
reduction, including over nonreduced local Artinian C-algebras. No mathematical
gap was found in the finite-free-strand argument. Details follow so that the
nilpotent-base justification is independently reviewable.

1. The determinantal chart is genuinely a rank149 chart over an arbitrary ring
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let M be the1050x150 centrality matrix over a local ring A. Suppose a149x149
minor is invertible and all150x150 minors vanish. Gaussian row/column operations
over A put M into the form diag(I149, residual_column), with the residual column
in the remaining901 rows. Each entry of that column is, up to a unit, one of
the150x150 minors obtained by adjoining one row to the chosen149-row block.
Consequently the residual column is zero over A itself, not only modulo the
nilradical. The kernel is a free rank-one direct summand. Its generator P can
be normalized to have one coefficient equal to1. The equation M(F)P=0 is exact.
All these operations commute with base change on the selected minor chart.

2. Finite-free complex lemma, including nilpotent bases
------------------------------------------------------
Let (A,m,k) be a local ring and C_n→...→C_1→C_0 a bounded complex of finite
free A-modules. Assume the reduced complex over k has H_i=0 for every i>0.
Then C is homotopy equivalent to a finite free A-module concentrated in degree0.
In particular it is exact in positive degrees and H_0(C) is finite free.

Proof: at the largest index n with C_n!=0, the reduced differential is
injective. A full-column-size minor is nonzero modulo m and therefore a unit
in A. Elementary operations split the map as an identity onto a direct summand
of C_(n-1). The complex relation makes the next differential zero on this
summand, so this is a contractible direct summand of the entire complex.
Remove it and repeat. Reduction of the remaining complex is still acyclic in
positive degrees. At the end only a free degree-zero module remains. If a
source has rank zero, that stage is simply omitted. This proof requires no
reducedness, no flatness hypothesis on an initially specified cokernel, and
no assumption that A is a domain.

3. Application to every graded Buchsbaum–Eisenbud strand
-------------------------------------------------------
For b=p(P), the Pfaffian identities make
 0→S_A(-10) --b--> S_A(-6)^5 --P--> S_A(-4)^5 --b^t--> S_A
an actual complex over A[x0,...,x4]. Each homogeneous strand is a bounded
complex of finite free A-modules. On the residue field, the prescribed normal
quintic / nonzero-Pfaffian conditions give a height-three Pfaffian ideal.
The field-level theorem and Buchsbaum–Eisenbud therefore make every residue
strand exact in positive homological degrees. The lemma applies separately
to every strand. Their direct sum is the required exact graded complex over A.
There is no passage from a finite jet to all orders here: every degree is
covered by the same finite-free lemma.

For a residue field not algebraically closed, one may first extend it to its
algebraic closure, apply the field theorem there, and descend exactness along
that faithfully flat extension. The normal-hypersurface open locus means
geometric normality of these fibers. Thus closed complex points are not an
unstated restriction on the family argument.

4. Unit proportionality is exact over the base
----------------------------------------------
Write f=gradient(F). The equation Pf=0 places the vector f, whose entries have
degree4, in degree10 of the kernel at S_A(-6)^5. Exactness identifies that
kernel with the image of S_A(-10); the degree10 part of its source is A.
Therefore f=c b for c in A. Modulo m this is the nonzero proportionality
constant from the field theorem, so c is a unit. This avoids fraction-field
arguments over a ring with zero divisors. It also does not require taking a
square root of c: the desired objects and the degeneracy map are projective.

5. Divergence and Jacobi impose no hidden additional equations on this chart
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Differentiating Pf=0 in the polynomial coordinates, the symmetric Hessian
cancels against P and gives (div P) dot f=0 over A. Since f=c b and c is a unit,
div P is a syzygy among b with coefficients of degree1. Exactness in degree5
at S_A(-4)^5 says that no such syzygy exists: its preceding module
S_A(-6)^5 has degree5 part zero. Hence div P=0 over A, including nilpotents.

All derivatives here are relative polynomial derivatives d_x, so elements
of A are constants. Thus alpha_P=sum b_i dx_i=c^(-1)d_xF is closed. The BV
identity, which is a polynomial identity with rational coefficients, now gives
[P,P]=0 over A. Euler's formula gives
 F_P=(1/5)sum x_i b_i=c^(-1)F.
The recovered projective degeneration form is therefore exactly [F].

6. The converse over nonreduced bases
-------------------------------------
The canonical divergence-free lift is defined by a linear splitting with
constant rational coefficients, so it is available after every base change.
Its equivalence with the projective Poisson equations also persists over A:
if a divergence-free lift has Jacobiator E wedge Q, applying divergence gives
5Q-E wedge divQ=0, and wedging with E forces E wedge Q=0. Thus its Jacobiator
vanishes. Conversely affine Jacobi plainly implies the projective equations.

For a Poisson family whose degeneracy hypersurface is normal, p(P)=gradient(F_P)
and Pp(P)=0 are polynomial identities. Locally some coefficient of the
projective lift P is a unit. Elementary column operations can therefore make
P a standard basis vector; M(F)P=0 then forces all maximal150-minors to vanish
over A, not just at points. On every residue field the centrality matrix has
rank149 by the field theorem, so some149-minor is locally invertible. These
charts cover the family, and their kernel line is the original Poisson line.

7. Gluing, projective scaling, and scope
---------------------------------------
Replacing F by a unit multiple does not change the kernel line of M(F).
Replacing P by a unit u multiplies its Pfaffian vector and F_P by u^2; this
does not change the corresponding point of projective quintic space. The two
constructions thus glue, commute with base change, and are mutually inverse
as morphisms of the stated open schemes. No additional square-root choice or
reduction operation is involved.

The theorem describes the full normal-degeneracy locus as the open part of
the ACTUAL maximal-minor determinantal scheme, further restricted by the
149-minor and nonzero-Pfaffian open conditions. It is not a statement about
the reduced determinantal variety and it does not assert that this scheme
is reduced. Nor does it enumerate the irreducible components of that locus.
The later tangent-injectivity assertion is compatible with this: fixing the
degeneration form leaves a reduced point, while nilpotent directions that
move the degeneration form are retained on both sides of the isomorphism.
