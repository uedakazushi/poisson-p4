A. Extension classification (sections 4--5 of the rank2 draft)
-------------------------------------------------------------
The displayed S,w resolve I_Z(2) for Z=V(a,b) union V(c,d). Direct
verification gives (ac,ad,bc,bd) S=0 and S w=0. This is the tensor-product
resolution of the two disjoint-variable ideals (a,b) and (c,d).

The global extension group is computed by applying Hom(-,O) to that
locally free resolution. All relevant positive-degree line bundles have
no higher cohomology on P4. Thus Ext1 is the kernel of the linear-form
syzygy map f -> f w, modulo the four constant combinations of rows of S.
The dimensions are 6 and 4, respectively. The two displayed representatives
  (a,0,0,b), (0,c,d,0)
are independent modulo those rows, so all extensions have
  f=(lambda a, mu c, mu d, lambda b).

There is a particularly transparent local check of reflexivity. Where
c is a unit, eliminate the second and fourth generators using the first
and fourth relations. The local presentation becomes three generators
with the single relation
  (b,-a,mu c).
At the generic point of V(a,b), this module is free exactly when mu!=0.
For mu=0 it is O direct-sum (a,b), which is not reflexive over the
regular local ring of dimension two. Similarly, where a is a unit the
remaining relation is (d,-c,lambda a), forcing lambda!=0 at the generic
point of V(c,d). Hence reflexivity implies lambda*mu!=0.

Conversely, when both parameters are nonzero the sheaf is free away from
the common point. The length-two free resolution gives depth two at that
point; together with torsion-freeness this proves reflexivity. Necessity
alone already suffices for the exhaustion argument.

Under (a,b)->r(a,b), (c,d)->s(c,d), the columns of S are multiplied by
(s,r,r,s). Dividing the relation columns by these factors restores S and
changes the extension parameters to
  (lambda r/s, mu s/r).
Scaling the fifth generator scales both parameters together. Nonzero
lambda,mu can therefore both be normalized to one over C.

The extension gives h0(E)=1+h0(I_Z(2))=5. Dualizing and twisting yields
F=ker(T^t:O(1)^5 -> O(2)^4). Direct calculation confirms T^t P_geo=0,
and all five Pfaffians of P_geo vanish. Its ten entries form a basis of
Sym^2(C4)^*, with basis determinant 2 in the monomial order of the script.
In particular they have no common divisor. Thus P_geo is the primitive
Plucker generator of det(F)=O, up to scalar.

D. Total rank-at-most-two coverage, including divisorial zeros
-------------------------------------------------------------
For nonzero rank-two pi with no divisorial zero, det(T_F)=O and the linear
bundle isomorphism wedge2 T(P4)=Omega^2(P4)(5) identifies its projective
class with its degree-two, dimension-two foliation. Integrability and
decomposability on the foliation side are exactly the rank-two Poisson
conditions. There is no extra multiplier: H0(det(T_F))=C.

The stable branch has now been exhausted by the new sheaf proof above
and the c2=2,3 proof. Corrrea--Muniz Theorem 4.1 treats the remaining
strictly semistable and unstable cases. Its affine-Lie branch is reduced
by Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.3 to the three nonexceptional partitions of 5:
  (5), (4,1), (3,2).
The partitions (3,1,1) and (2,2,1) are in Log1112. Partition (2,1,1,1)
has a rank-one nilpotent field x0 partial1, so its wedge has a divisorial
factor. The all-ones partition does not define an affine-Lie family;
commutative actions are covered by Log11111.

Relevant root-openable refs: turn271view0, turn271view1, turn271view2.
The paper has off-by-one printed indices in the Rat labels in Theorem4.1

Now let pi have a divisorial zero of degree e>0. The saturated foliation
has degree d=2-e, so (e,d) is (2,0) or (1,1). The standard degree-zero and
degree-one foliation classification gives the three cases below.

(i) d=0: its dual form is
  omega=Q i_E(dL0 wedge dL1 wedge dL2).
Dividing by Q L0 L1 L2 gives a horizontal decomposable logarithmic 2-form
with poles among L0,L1,L2,Q. This is a special residue point in Log1112.

(ii) d=1 rational type112: its dual form is
  omega=H i_E(dL0 wedge dL1 wedge dQ).
Dividing by H L0 L1 Q again gives a special Log1112 residue point. In each
case, horizontal residues lie in wedge2(C3), hence all are decomposable;
perturbing residues preserves rank <=2. Degenerate quadrics or arrangements
are allowed by closure of the same irreducible polynomial parameter family.

(iii) d=1 linear pullback: with fixed projection center e, write
  pi=H partial_e wedge v_A,
where A is an arbitrary linear endomorphism of C5/Ce. The parameter space
(H,A) is irreducible, and EVERY member is rank <=2 and Poisson. On its
dense open H(e)!=0, A regular semisimple, a coordinate change gives

This is in the rank-two toric family. Its special divisorial zero can be
removed by varying the two diagonal fields in the same Cartan subalgebra,
while keeping their wedge decomposable. Thus these points lie in the
closure of Log11111 WITHIN the rank-at-most-two locus. All (H,A) follow
by closure of the same irreducible family. No rank-four toric deformation
is used for this coverage statement.

Consequently the reduced projective rank-at-most-two locus is the union

There is no uncovered divisorial stratum. The zero affine bivector is
