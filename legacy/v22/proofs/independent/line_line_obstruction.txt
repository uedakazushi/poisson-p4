LINE-LINE INTERSECTION OBSTRUCTION (v22, corrected proof; m=2 independently audited)

Hypotheses: actual normal Poisson quintic D in P4; each component of reduced singular support generically E8; L,L' distinct line components meeting at p. Modular residue vanishes at p on both. Use actual affine Poisson tensor pi and affine coordinate volume; Z=div(pi), div Z=0, [Z,pi]=0, Z(F)=0 for F=Pf(pi). The claim is that both residue slopes at p vanish. Hence any line meeting a second line at a point where its residue has simple zero is excluded.

1. GLOBAL LINE FLAGS AND DEGREE BOUNDS.
Choose homogeneous coordinates (s,t,u,v,w) with L=(u=v=w=0). The conormal bracket matrix B(s,t) is symmetric (modular tangency) and entries are linear in s,t. Generic E8 says B has rank1 and is nonzero. A symmetric rank1 matrix pencil has form ell(s,t) vv^T for constant nonzero v; proof via all matrices in pencil rank1, sum of two rank1 symmetric matrices of independent images would have rank2. Thus after constant normal-coordinate change, Hessian of F along L is H(s,t) w^2. Pfaffian differentiation gives H proportional to R(s,t) ell(s,t), where R is modular residue quadratic on P1. A choice of scale does not matter. Hence at a simple modular zero with slope alpha !=0, h=ord H is 1 or2.

On the constant Hessian kernel (u,v), the cubic jet of F has coefficients homogeneous degree2 in s,t. It is a nonzero cube at the generic E8 point. Its projective cube direction defines a morphism P1->P1 after removing common zero factor; Veronese degree3 forces degree0 since total degree2<3. Thus the cube direction is also constant, and its scalar coefficient has zero order m in {0,1,2} at p.

From Z(F)=0, the quadratic normal jet and cubic jet on its radical are preserved. At p, Z1 has invariant flag in normal cotangent space whose first/second one-dimensional factors have eigenvalues -h alpha/2 and -m alpha/3. This follows by comparing the leading coefficient in t in the identities R H'+2 a_H H=0 and R C'+3 a_C C=0. The remaining eigenvalue follows from div Z=0. Consequently the ambient eigenvalue multiset is
 alpha*S(h,m), S(h,m)={1,-h/2,-m/3,h/2+m/3-1}.
This statement uses finite jets only, no formal normal form. Intrinsic tangent and ambient eigenvalues agree.

If the residue slope on a line is zero, R vanishes to order2 (nonzero R), while both flags are still defined and their scalar coefficients have finite vanishing order. The same leading-coefficient identities force both normal flag eigenvalues to be zero; trace zero forces the remaining normal eigenvalue zero. Together with tangent slope0, all ambient eigenvalues are0. Thus if one incident line has nonzero slope, so does the other.

For two nonzero slopes alpha,beta, both eigenvalue multisets above must coincide. Exhaustive rational comparison of the six possible S(h,m) shows only beta/alpha=1 (same h,m) or beta/alpha=-1 with h=h'=2 and m=m'. The eigenvalue1 in every S has multiplicity1, so beta/alpha=1 contradicts the independent tangent eigenvectors of the two lines. Hence beta=-alpha, h=h'=2, and equal m in {0,1,2}. In particular both conormal pencils vanish at p, their Hessian directions at p are the other line tangent directions. Choose affine axes x,y for L,L', normal coordinates z,w. Both zero conormal brackets imply pi_1=ell(z,w) d_x wedge d_y.

2. CASE m=0.
Normal Z1 eigenvalues are0,0. If ell=0, pi_1=0, so F has order>=4 and its transverse cubic scalar vanishes at p, contradicting m=0.
If ell!=0, the first-order coefficient of the conormal pencil along x is nonzero and centered in the other tangent direction y. Thus pi_2^{zw} has a nonzero xy coefficient i (choose normal coordinates determinant appropriately). Then F_3=ell*pi_2^{zw} contains i xy ell, producing a nonzero mixed Hessian term of order1 along L. Other terms cannot cancel this monomial because pi_1 has only its xy entry. This contradicts h=2, which means all quadratic normal Hessian coefficients vanish to order2. Thus m=0 impossible.

3. CASE m=1.
Scale alpha=3; Z1 weights are (3,-3,-1,1) on (x,y,z,w). These are distinct. Invariance [Z1,pi_1]=0 forces ell=0. Thus pi starts in degree2, [Z1,pi_2]=0 and [pi_2,pi_2]=0. The complete weight-invariant quadratic tensor is
 Pxy=a xy+b zw,
 Pxz=c xz+d w^2,
 Pxw=e xw,
 Pyz=f yz,
 Pyw=g yw+h z^2,
 Pzw=i xy+j zw.
Here i!=0 because the conormal pencil vanishes simply at p. The cubic normal terms of F_4 along x and y are respectively -ch*x*z^3 and -dg*y*w^3; m=1 forces d,h nonzero. The xyz Jacobi component has coefficients bi at xyz and b(c+f+j)-2dh at wz^2. Thus bi=0 forces b=0, then dh=0. Contradiction.

4. CASE m=2 (CORRECTED PROJECTIVE ARGUMENT).
Scale alpha=3; Z1 has distinct weights (3,-3,-2,2). As above ell=0, so pi starts at degree2. Choose LINEAR eigen-coordinates preserving the two axes and their constant Hessian/cubic flags. This is possible since the four eigenvalues are distinct: Hessian directions are respectively y and x, cubic directions on their radicals are z and w. Consequently the leading transverse cubic coefficients require NONZERO x^2*z^3 and y^2*w^3 coefficients in F5. Both monomials have weight0.

Projectivity rules this out. In an affine chart, the canonical divergence-free quadratic lift gives
 pi_3=(1/5) E wedge Z_2,
where E=sum x_i d_i and Z=div(pi). Indeed pi_ij=P_ij-x_i P_0j+x_j P_0i, and direct differentiation using delta P=0 and homogeneity gives Z_j=-5 P_0j. Take degree3/degree2 parts to obtain the identity. It remains valid after all linear coordinate changes.

Since pi_1=0, [Z,pi]=0 in degree2 implies [Z1,pi2]=0. The weights (3,-3,-2,2) have NO resonant quadratic vector field: no degree2 monomial has any of these four weights. Thus Z2 has no weight0 component as a vector field. Since E has weight0, pi3 has no weight0 bivector component. Since pi2 is invariant and tr(Z1)=0, the coefficient F5 of the mixed Pfaffian pi2 wedge pi3 has no weight0 monomial. This contradicts the two necessary nonzero weight0 cubic-flag coefficients above.

This proof uses no formal coordinate change and no assumption that the xyzw coefficient in F4 vanishes. That coefficient is unrestricted at this stage because it contains a Hessian variable on each line.

CONCLUSION: two intersecting E8 line components cannot have nonzero modular slope at their intersection. Any such intersection has double modular zero on both lines.
