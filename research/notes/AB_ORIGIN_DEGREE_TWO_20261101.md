# Degree two of the normalization of AB

Date: 2026-11-01. This is a bounded graded-generation result, not a proof of normality at the origin.

Let B=C[AB], with each quadratic-tensor coefficient assigned degree one, and let D be its normalization. Then D1=B1 has dimension45 and

\[
D_2=B_2,\qquad \dim D_2=761.
\]

Thus no new normalization generator occurs in degree two. Whether all higher-degree functions are generated by B1 remains open. The prior local theorems prove normality away from the origin but do not, by themselves, answer this graded-generation question.

## 1. The homogeneous bundle calculation

Use the normal proper birational incidence X→AB from `AB_INCIDENCE_20261101.md`, and its projection pi:X→LG(2,4). Since AB is affine, D=H0(X,O_X). The fiber grading gives

\[
D_n=H^0(\mathrm{LG}(2,4),\mathcal E_n),
\qquad \mathcal E_n=(\pi_*\mathcal O_X)_n.
\]

Each E_n is a homogeneous vector bundle: in every fixed-plane chart the fiber coordinate ring is C[Y]⊗C[g0,g1,g2], where Y is the rank-one traceless 3 by3 matrix cone. Changes of the complementary Lagrangian plane act linearly on the core N and add a linear expression in N to the vertical coordinates g. Consequently the filtration by number of vertical factors is parabolic-invariant. It is a filtration, not in general a direct sum of homogeneous bundles.

Write (a,b) for the bundle Sigma^(a,b) U*, where U is the tautological rank-two bundle; negative b is allowed. The core degree-one Levi module is (3,-1)⊕(2,0), and the vertical module is (3,1). In degree two the three associated-graded pieces are:

| Piece | Levi bundle types, with multiplicities |
|---|---|
| core ring C[Y]2 | (6,-2), (5,-1), 2(4,0), (2,2) |
| core times vertical | (6,0), 2(5,1), 2(4,2), (3,3) |
| vertical squared | (6,2), (4,4) |

The code verifies the first row by computing the weight ranks of all nine quadratic minors inside Sym² of the eight core coordinates. The quotient has dimension27. The other rows are checked by exact GL2 character multiplication.

For cohomology use the Lagrangian Borel–Weil–Bott theorem with rho=(2,1): add rho, test whether the absolute values are distinct and nonzero, sort absolute values and subtract rho. The unique nonzero higher-cohomology term here is H1 of (6,-2), equal to Sym6(V) of dimension84; (5,-1) is acyclic. All other entries have H0 only. The convention and theorem are stated explicitly in [Fonarev, Theorem3.2](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=9880&wshow=paper), applied there to U and here to its dual.

The resulting dimensions are

| Piece | h0 | h1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| core ring | 84 | 84 |
| core times vertical | 486 | 0 |
| vertical squared | 275 | 0 |

Adding h0 to obtain845 would be incorrect: the parabolic extension has a nonzero connecting map. Its nonvanishing is proved next.

The same degree-one computation is uncomplicated: (3,-1) is acyclic, (2,0) has ten sections, and (3,1) has35, with no higher cohomology. Hence h0(E1)=45. The actual coefficient functions have rank45 on a dominant incidence chart, so D1=B1.

## 2. The connecting map is an isomorphism on Sym6(V)

Let P be the parabolic stabilizing L=span(∂y,∂w). In coordinates q=(x,z), p=(y,w), its Levi acts by diag(T,T^-t), and its unipotent radical includes p↦p+Cq with C symmetric.

For clarity, the following weight calculation uses pullback of functions by the point transformations; replacing these by inverse transformations changes both representations simultaneously and gives the usual P-module convention. Under T=diag(a,b), the coordinate h0 of H=h0*x^4+... has weight(-3,1), and the coefficient g0 of x² in the vertical quadratic has weight(-3,-1). The degree-two fiber coordinate ring therefore has a unique vector of weight(-6,0), namely h0*g0. The core-square and vertical-square pieces have total weights -4 and -8 and cannot contribute to that weight.

Suppose a copy of the irreducible Sp4 module Sym6(V) occurred in H0(E2). Evaluation at the fixed plane, or Frobenius reciprocity for homogeneous bundles, gives a nonzero P-map

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^6(V)\longrightarrow (\mathcal E_2)_L.
\]

The vector y^6 has weight(-6,0), so its image must be c*h0*g0. Moreover y^6 is P-cyclic: the P orbit of the linear form y contains a dense open subset of V* (its p part is nonzero and the symmetric shear can supply any q part), and sixth powers of a dense set of linear forms span Sym6(V*), which is self-dual as an Sp4 module.

Now use the shear w↦w+u z. It fixes y^6. For the tensor chart

\[
F=qS/4+J_2\nabla H,\qquad A=DF,
\]

the mixed block A is unchanged and the vertical block changes by C A−A^t C. With C=diag(0,u), its upper-right entry is -u A21. The x² coefficient of A21 is -12h0. Thus

\[
g_0\longmapsto g_0+12u h_0,
\qquad h_0\longmapsto h_0.
\]

The candidate image has infinitesimal variation 12c*h0². This is nonzero when c is nonzero: h0² does not vanish on Y, as witnessed by N=E13. Equivariance forces c=0, contradicting cyclicity and a nonzero P-map. Therefore Sym6(V) does not occur in H0(E2).

Let F0⊂F1⊂E2 be the vertical-degree filtration. The only possible connecting map from H0(F1/F0) to H1(F0) is between their unique Sym6(V) summands. If it were zero, that summand would occur in H0(F1), hence in H0(E2), contradicting the preceding argument. It is thus a nonzero scalar isomorphism on the84-dimensional irreducible module. The exact cohomology sequences give

\[
h^0(F_1)=486,\quad H^1(F_1)=0,
\quad h^0(\mathcal E_2)=486+275=761,
\quad H^1(\mathcal E_2)=0.
\]

This proves the characteristic-zero upper bound independently of the modular matrix computation below.

## 3. Actual coordinate products attain that dimension

The certificate parametrizes a dense chart of X by

\[
N=(1,a,b)^t(-ad-be,d,e),
\]

three upper-shear coordinates for LG and three vertical coefficients. This gives ten parameters. The full quadratic tensor is reconstructed and pushed through the symplectic shear. Its sixty raw coefficients span45 independent functions. Products of a selected basis give1035 candidate degree-two functions.

Sparse elimination modulo32003 finds a761 by761 nonzero minor in their coefficient matrix. The report saves all selected coordinate indices, products and pivot monomials, and the nonzero triangular pivot product11854. Every rational coefficient denominator is checked to be invertible modulo32003.

This is used only as a rational rank lower bound: a minor nonzero after a permitted reduction is nonzero over QQ. It does not provide a rational rank upper bound, ideal equality or all-degree result by itself. Combining rank at least761 with the characteristic-zero bound dim D2=761 proves that these products span D2. As they belong to B2, equality B2=D2 follows.

## 4. Reproduction and remaining problem

`code/ab_origin_degree_two_20261101.py --output <new-path>` produces `reports/AB_ORIGIN_DEGREE_TWO_20261101.json`. The script checks the Levi decompositions, Bott dimensions, exact unipotent identity, nonzero core witness and modular minor. The parabolic-extension and reciprocity proof in §2 is an essential part of the result.

The actual remaining problem at the origin is all-degree generation of D by D1, or an explicit extra generator in a higher degree. Neither normality away from the vertex nor the equality through degree two implies the answer. The calculation gives a verified starting point and records why ignoring the nontrivial bundle extension would have produced a false dimension845.
